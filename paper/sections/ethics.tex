\section{Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:ethics}

\textbf{Representativeness and bias.} RUGD covers a small set of parks, trails,
a creek, and a village area, recorded by one small robot whose camera sits
under 25\,cm above the ground~\cite{wigness2019rugd}. Season, geography,
weather, and time of day are therefore narrowly sampled. Snow, mud, desert,
night, and heavy rain are absent or rare, and RUGD has no \emph{mud} class at
all. A model trained here may look reliable on similar trails yet fail
silently on terrain, vegetation, or viewpoints it has never seen, such as the
much higher camera of a full-size vehicle. A segmentation model has no
demographic attributes in the usual sense, but the same principle applies: the
model's error rates differ across environments, and the environments that are
under-represented in the data are also the ones with the least evidence about
its behavior. We report per-class and per-sequence results, not only mIoU, so
that these disparities stay visible.

\textbf{Safety.} The costliest errors in this task are false negatives on
hazards: water labeled as drivable ground, or a log absorbed into the
surrounding grass. These are also among the rarest classes, so a model can
reach a high mIoU while still missing them. Our models are research artifacts
evaluated offline on a fixed benchmark. They are \emph{not} safety systems and
should not be used to make driving decisions without independent validation,
calibrated uncertainty, redundant sensing (\eg LiDAR), and a conservative
planner. For the same reason, any driver-assist use of this kind of model
should present it as advisory only.

\textbf{Dual use.} Both datasets were produced with U.S. Army Research
Laboratory involvement~\cite{wigness2019rugd,jiang2021rellis}, and off-road
autonomy has clear military applications alongside search-and-rescue,
agriculture, and planetary exploration. Terrain segmentation is a
general-purpose perception capability rather than a weapon, but we note this
dual-use context explicitly and limit our work to openly published methods and
data.

\textbf{Licensing and privacy.} RELLIS-3D is released under the Creative
Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 license~\cite{jiang2021rellis}.
Any model weights or derived data that touch it are therefore for
non-commercial research use and must be shared under the same terms. We use
RUGD under its authors' research-use distribution and do not redistribute
either dataset's images. RUGD includes a \emph{person} class, so some frames
show people. Our figures avoid identifiable individuals. Photographs we took
for the demo have their EXIF and GPS metadata removed and are framed to exclude
people and license plates.

actually emerged and how we handled each.
